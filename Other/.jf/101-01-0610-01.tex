\section*{Lines in \(\mathbb{R}^3\)}
A line in \(\mathbb{R}^3\) is completely determined by a point and a direction vector

\subsubsection*{Vector Equation}
Let \(\overrightarrow{0P_0}\) be the vector joing the origin in \(\mathbb{R}^3\) to the point \(P_0\)

The vector equation of the line through \(P_0\) with direction vector \(\vec{v}\) is given by
\[
\vec{r}(t) = \overrightarrow{0P_0} + t\vec{v}, \quad  \quad t \in \mathbb{R}
\]

All vectors that satisfy the equation have their tips lying on the line.

As \(t\) ranges from \(-\infty\) to \(\infty\) the tips of the vectors trace out the line

\subsubsection*{With Coordinates}
Let \(P_0 = (x_0,y_0,z_0)\), \(\vec{v} = \langle a,b,c \rangle\)


\begin{align*}
    \vec{r}(t) &= \langle x_0,y_0,z_0 \rangle + t\langle a,b,c \rangle \\
    &= \langle x_0 + ta, y_0 + tb, z_0 +tc \rangle
\end{align*}

A vector \(\langle x,y,z \rangle \in \mathbb{R}^3\) lies on the line if and only if
\[
x = x_0  + ta 
\]
\[
y = y_0 + tb
\]
\[
z = z_0 + tc
\]

\section*{Planes in \(\mathbb{R}^3\)}
A plane in \(\mathbb{R}^3\) is completely determined by a point and a normal vector (perpendicular to the plane)

\subsubsection*{Equation}
We wish to describe the plane in \(\mathbb{R}^3\) that passes through the point \(P_0(x_0,y_0,z_0)\)
and that has \(\vec{n} \langle a,b,c \rangle\) as the normal vector

Let \(P(x,y,x)\) be any other point in the plane

Let \(\overrightarrow{PP_0}\) be the vector that joins \(P\) to \(P_0\)

\(\overrightarrow{PP_0}\) lies in the plane

Thus \(\overrightarrow{PP_0} \cdot \vec{n} = 0\)

\[
\langle x - x_0, y-y_0,z-z_0 \rangle \cdot \langle a,b,c \rangle = 0
\]
\[
ax+by+cz = ax_0 + by_0 +cz_0
\]

\textbf{Note}

In this form the coeffecients of \(x,y,z\) form the components of the normal vector
